Moved, not yet integratedThis is the impedance-curve and self-resonance material taken out of Module 3. The frequency-domain figure that used to sit under Module 3's decoupling plot is not reproduced here; Module 4's impedance widget replaces it. The explanation and the question are kept so the content is not lost.

A bigger capacitor isn't automatically better, because a real capacitor is a capacitor in series with its ESR and its ESL. At low frequency the capacitance dominates and the impedance falls as frequency rises. At the self-resonant frequency (SRF) the capacitance and the ESL cancel, leaving only the ESR. Above it, the ESL dominates and the part behaves as an inductor:

$$f_\text{SRF} = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{L_\text{ESL}\,C}}$$

A chip's current bursts have most of their energy at tens to hundreds of megahertz, usually above the SRF of every capacitor on the board. Up there, what decides how well a capacitor decouples is its inductance: its own ESL, which grows with package size, plus the trace and vias connecting it to the pin. That's why a 100 nF capacitor in a small package right at the pin beats a 10 µF part in a larger package, and why a capacitor a centimetre away does little for fast edges whatever its value. Larger capacitors still have a job: they cover the slower, larger demands that the small ones run out of charge for, so boards use both. Combining values brings its own problems (parallel capacitors can resonate with each other), which Module 12 covers along with placement.

The mirror image for an inductor: the turns of a winding have capacitance between them. Above its self-resonant frequency an inductor behaves as a capacitor.

Design exerciseSwapping in a bigger decoupling capacitor

A chip has a 100 nF 0402 decoupling capacitor right at its power pin. To "add more capacitance", someone replaces it with a 10 µF 1206 in the same spot.

Does the chip's supply get cleaner during its fast current bursts?

  1. Much cleaner: it's a hundred times the capacitance.
    At the frequencies in a fast edge, capacitance isn't what limits a decoupling capacitor.
  2. No better, and probably worse: above their self-resonant frequency both parts act as inductors, and the larger 1206 has more inductance.
    For fast edges, the inductance of the part and its connection decides.
  3. Exactly the same, because they're in the same position.
    Same position, but not the same package.
  4. Better only if it's an electrolytic.
    Electrolytics have even more inductance.

A chip's current bursts have most of their energy at tens to hundreds of megahertz, above the self-resonant frequency of both capacitors. There, each behaves as a small inductor: its ESL plus the trace and vias to the pin. The 1206 has more ESL than the 0402, so it does slightly worse on fast edges, despite its hundred times larger value. The 10 µF is still useful, for slower demands, alongside the 100 nF, not instead of it. Module 3's time-domain figure shows the same comparison as a deeper dip; this section is the frequency reason.
