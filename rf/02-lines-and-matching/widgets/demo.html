Try this: (1) Press "Short, then λ/4 of line". The dot travels half-way round the chart and ends at the open circuit on the right edge: a shorted quarter-wave line looks like an open. (2) Pick 100 Ω and slide the line length. The dot circles the center at a fixed distance: a lossless line never changes |Γ| or VSWR, only the angle. (3) Drag the dot into the top half: that's inductive. Bottom half is capacitive.

L-network matching designer

Enter what your source wants to see and what the load actually is. The designer finds every two-part (one series, one shunt) match and draws its path on the Smith chart.

Examples:

Try this: start with the 25 Ω → 50 Ω example: two answers, one low-pass (series L, shunt C) and one high-pass (series C, shunt L). Then pick "2 Ω PA output". The node Q jumps to about 5 and the 10 dB-return-loss bandwidth collapses. That's why big impedance ratios are usually matched in two or more smaller steps.
