# Recording outline — circuit fundamentals lab

Not published. Jekyll ignores files whose names start with `_`.

One recording, about 13 minutes. Say the resistor values and the arithmetic out loud. Write down the voltages you actually read. Do not aim at a scripted display.

## Lab — meter, divider, load

**Total: 13:00.**

Use a current-limited bench supply, three resistors that are about 10 kΩ, and the meter you have. If the meter manual does not say 10 MΩ, say the number it does say.

| Time | Duration | Shot / talking point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | 1:30 | On camera: do not put the current jacks across the supply. The meter on a current range is nearly a short. The fuse opens. Voltage is parallel. Resistance is unpowered. |
| 1:30 | 2:30 | Show the lead holes. Measure the supply on a DC voltage range. Turn the supply off and measure one 10 kΩ resistor. Say which range. Do not measure resistance with the supply on. |
| 4:00 | 3:00 | Build 10 kΩ over 10 kΩ from 5.0 V to 0 V. Current limit already set, tens of milliamps is plenty. Predict 2.50 V before you look. Then look. A 10 MΩ meter sags this divider by about 1 mV, which the display will not show. |
| 7:00 | 3:00 | Add 10 kΩ from the middle node to 0 V. Predict 1.667 V. Show the meter fall. If it does not, the third resistor is not in the circuit. |
| 10:00 | 2:00 | Say why the meter alone did not move the node and the resistor did. Optional: point at the LED story on the reading page. Do not destroy an LED on camera. |
| 12:00 | 1:00 | Takeaways. Point at the blank table on the reading page. |
