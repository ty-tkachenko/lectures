Design challenge:

A 12 V avionics rail needs a bleed resistor so a meter shows the rail is up, drawing about 10 mA. The resistor sits in a warm bay, so keep the heat at or under half of a 1/2 W rating.

  • Voltage across the resistor: 12 V
  • Current: 10 mA, with 8–15 mA acceptable
  • Power at or under 0.25 W

Produce: the resistance and the wattage rating. At 12 V that current is a 1.2 kΩ part dissipating 0.12 W, which is 24% of a 1/2 W rating. Check it in the widget above.

Hint

Start from I = V/R, then check P = V²/R. A smaller resistor draws more current and makes more heat. Half of 1/2 W is 0.25 W.

Check your design

R = 1.2 kΩ on a 1/2 W rating. I = 12/1200 = 10.0 mA and P = 12²/1200 = 0.120 W, which is 0.120/0.500 = 24% of the rating. The verdict stays inside the rating. About 800 Ω (15 mA, 0.18 W) through 1.5 kΩ (8 mA, 0.096 W) also meets the band.

Design challenge:

Bias this two-node front end from 12 V so node A sits at 6 V and node B at 3 V. Keep every branch current at or under 1 mA so a small sensor supply can feed it.

  • Source: 12 V
  • Node A: 6.0 V, node B: 3.0 V
  • Every branch current ≤ 1 mA

Produce: R1, R2, R3, and R4. One set that lands both nodes on the targets with room under 1 mA is 10 kΩ, 10 kΩ, 20 kΩ, and 10 kΩ. Check it in the widget above.

Hint

With R4 = R2, node B is half of node A when the currents balance. Set Va = 6 V so the drop on R1 is also 6 V, then pick R3 so the leftover current at node A equals Va/R3.

Check your design

R1 = R2 = R4 = 10 kΩ and R3 = 20 kΩ. Va = 6.00 V and Vb = 3.00 V. I1 = (12 − 6)/10 kΩ = 0.600 mA, I2 = (6 − 3)/10 kΩ = 0.300 mA, I3 = 6/20 kΩ = 0.300 mA, and I4 = 3/10 kΩ = 0.300 mA. The widget rounds those to 0.6 mA and 0.3 mA. KCL holds at both nodes, and the largest branch is 0.600 mA.

Design challenge:

A 12.6 V pack has to feed a 3.3 V sense pin through a divider. The pin looks like about 100 kΩ. Keep the unloaded output near 3.3 V and the sag under 1% once that pin is attached.

  • Input: 12.6 V, target unloaded output: 3.3 V ± 0.1 V
  • Load: 100 kΩ
  • Sag under 1% of the unloaded voltage

Produce: R1 and R2. A 2.8 kΩ over 1.0 kΩ divider lands at 3.32 V unloaded and sags only 0.024 V into 100 kΩ. Check it in the widget above.

Hint

The unloaded ratio is R2/(R1+R2) = 3.3/12.6. Sag stays small when R2 is far below the 100 kΩ load, so pick R2 near 1 kΩ and set R1 from the ratio.

Check your design

R1 = 2.8 kΩ, R2 = 1.0 kΩ, load = 100 kΩ. Unloaded, V = 12.6 × 1000/3800 = 3.316 V. With the load, R2 ∥ 100 kΩ = 990.1 Ω and V = 12.6 × 990.1/3790.1 = 3.292 V. Sag is 0.024 V, which is 0.73% of 3.316 V. The widget rounds that to 0.7%, under the 1% limit and under the widget's 5% warning.

Design challenge:

An indicator LED on a 9 V rail has a 2.0 V forward drop and a 10 mA target. Choose the next E24 value at or above the ideal resistor so the current does not exceed 10 mA, and keep that resistor under a 1/8 W rating.

  • Supply 9 V, forward drop 2.0 V, target 10 mA
  • E24 value at or above the ideal resistance
  • Resistor power under 0.125 W

Produce: the ideal resistance and the E24 part you would buy. The ideal value is 700 Ω; the next E24 up is 750 Ω, which draws 9.3 mA and only 0.065 W. Check it in the widget above.

Hint

R = (Vs − Vf)/I. Rounding down raises the current above the target. The widget's E12 nearest is the value that can do that. Power is (Vs − Vf) times the current of the part you actually fit.

Check your design

Ideal R = (9 − 2)/0.010 = 700 Ω. The next E24 at or above 700 Ω is 750 Ω, so I = 7/750 = 9.33 mA and P = 7 × 9.33 mA = 0.065 W, under 0.125 W. The E12 nearest is 680 Ω, and 7/680 = 10.3 mA, which overshoots the target. The verdict stays clear of the 1/8 W warning on the E24 part.
