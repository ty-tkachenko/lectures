Design challenge:

A 3.3 V rail at 150 mA has to come from a 3S pack, 12.6 V full and 9.0 V empty, through an LDO. Quiescent current is 5 mA, the package is 40 °C/W, ambient is 40 °C, and the junction limit is 110 °C. The dropout is 0.3 V. Stay under the junction limit at the full pack, where the heat is worst.

  • Vin 12.6 V down to 9.0 V, Vout 3.3 V, Iout 150 mA, Iq 5 mA
  • θJA 40 °C/W, ambient 40 °C, junction limit 110 °C
  • Headroom above the 0.3 V dropout at both ends of the pack

Produce: the dissipation and junction temperature at 12.6 V, and the same pair at 9.0 V. At a full pack the pass element makes 1.46 W and the junction reaches 98 °C, still under 110 °C. Check it in the widget above.

Hint

Heat is (Vin − Vout)·Iout plus Vin·Iq. Junction temperature is ambient plus that heat times θJA. The full pack is the hot case. Dropout only matters when Vin − Vout falls under 0.3 V.

Check your design

At 12.6 V, Pdiss = (12.6 − 3.3)×0.150 + 12.6×0.005 = 1.458 W and TJ = 40 + 1.458×40 = 98.3 °C, under 110 °C. Efficiency is 0.495/1.953 = 25.3%. At 9.0 V, Pdiss = (9.0 − 3.3)×0.150 + 9.0×0.005 = 0.900 W and TJ = 76.0 °C. Headroom is 9.3 V and 5.7 V, both above 0.3 V, so the verdict stays clear.

Design challenge:

A buck drops a full 3S pack, 12.6 V, to 3.3 V at 300 mA. Keep it in continuous conduction with inductor ripple under 0.20 A, and keep the output ripple under 20 mV with a 22 µF capacitor at 20 mΩ ESR.

  • Vin 12.6 V, Vout 3.3 V, load 300 mA
  • Continuous conduction, ΔIL under 0.20 A
  • ΔVout under 20 mV with Cout = 22 µF and ESR = 20 mΩ

Produce: inductance and switching frequency. 22 µH at 800 kHz gives ΔIL = 0.138 A, a valley of 0.231 A, and about 3.8 mV of ripple. Check it in the widget above.

Hint

Duty is Vout/Vin. ΔIL = (Vin − Vout)·D/(L·fsw). The valley is Iout − ΔIL/2, and continuous conduction means that valley stays above zero. Ripple voltage is ΔIL/(8·fsw·C) plus ΔIL·ESR.

Check your design

L = 22 µH, fsw = 800 kHz, Cout = 22 µF, ESR = 20 mΩ. D = 3.3/12.6 = 0.262. ΔIL = (12.6 − 3.3)×0.262/(22 µH × 800 kHz) = 0.138 A. Valley = 0.300 − 0.069 = 0.231 A, so the mode is CCM. ΔVout ≈ 0.138/(8 × 800 kHz × 22 µF) + 0.138×0.020 Ω = 3.75 mV, under 20 mV. The verdict says continuous conduction.

Design challenge:

A 3.3 V rail at 200 mA runs from a 3S pack, 12.6 V full and 9.0 V empty. The LDO's quiescent current is 5 mA. The buck is estimated at 85% efficiency. Pick the one whose heat stays under 0.5 W across that pack.

  • Vout 3.3 V, load 200 mA, Iq 5 mA, buck efficiency 85%
  • Check both 12.6 V and 9.0 V
  • Chosen regulator's heat under 0.5 W at both voltages

Produce: LDO or buck, with the heat of each at 12.6 V. The linear regulator makes 1.92 W there. The buck makes 0.12 W and stays under half a watt for the whole pack. Check it in the widget above.

Hint

LDO heat is (Vin − Vout)·Iout + Vin·Iq, so it grows with the drop. Buck heat in this widget is Pout/η − Pout, which does not depend on Vin. The verdict flips once LDO heat crosses 0.5 W.

Check your design

Choose the buck. At 12.6 V the LDO dissipates (12.6 − 3.3)×0.200 + 12.6×0.005 = 1.923 W. Buck output power is 3.3×0.200 = 0.660 W, input is 0.660/0.85 = 0.776 W, and heat is 0.116 W. At 9.0 V the LDO is still (9.0 − 3.3)×0.200 + 9.0×0.005 = 1.185 W. The verdict at 12.6 V says to pick a buck.
