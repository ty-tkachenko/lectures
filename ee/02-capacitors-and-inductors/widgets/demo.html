Try this: (1) The defaults are 10 kΩ and 100 nF, so τ is 1 ms, on a 100 Hz square wave. (2) Raise the frequency until half a period is shorter than τ. (3) The rounding is the circuit refusing to jump. Five time constants is close enough for most resets.
Design challenge:

A 10 kΩ reset pull-up has to charge a capacitor from a 10 Hz square wave and still reach the rails. Five time constants should fit inside one half-period, with τ itself around 8–10 ms.

  • R = 10 kΩ, square wave 10 Hz, amplitude 5 V
  • τ between 8 ms and 10 ms
  • Half a period longer than 5τ, so the trace reaches the rail

Produce: the capacitance. 820 nF with 10 kΩ gives τ = 8.2 ms, and 50 ms of half-period still covers 5τ. Check it in the widget above.

Hint

τ = RC. Half a period at 10 Hz is 50 ms, so 5τ has to stay under 50 ms, which means τ under 10 ms. The stall verdict appears once half a period drops under 5τ.

Check your design

C = 820 nF. τ = 10 kΩ × 820 nF = 8.20 ms. The frequency slider lands at 10.05 Hz, so a half-period is 49.8 ms and 5τ is 41.0 ms. The rail readout says yes, and the verdict does not call it stalled. 1 µF would put 5τ on a 50 ms half-period, which is not longer, so that rail readout stays no.

Try this: (1) Leave the coil at 1 mH and the current at 200 mA. Turn the diode off and read the model spike for a 1 µs turn-off. (2) Turn the diode on and compare that spike with supply plus one diode drop. (3) Stretch the turn-off time and watch the undamped spike fall.
Design challenge:

A 10 mH relay coil carries 100 mA from a 5 V rail and the switch opens in about 1 µs. Without a catch diode the model spike is a kilovolt. Fit the diode so the switch only ever sees about one diode drop above the rail.

  • L = 10 mH, I = 100 mA, Vs = 5 V, turn-off = 1 µs
  • Diode on
  • Clamped voltage at Vs + 0.7 V, not the undamped L·I/Δt spike

Produce: diode on or off, and the voltage the switch must survive. With the diode on that voltage is 5.7 V instead of 1000 V. Check it in the widget above.

Hint

The undamped model is V = L·I/Δt. The diode clamps to the supply plus its own forward drop, about 0.7 V. Turn the diode on before you read the clamp.

Check your design

Diode on, L = 10 mH, I = 100 mA, Δt = 1 µs, Vs = 5 V. Without the diode, V = 0.010 × 0.100 / 1 µs = 1000 V. With it, the clamp is 5 + 0.7 = 5.7 V. The current then decays through the diode in about L·I/Vf = 1.43 ms. The verdict reports the clamp, not the kilovolt spike.

Try this: (1) Set R to 1.6 kΩ and C to 100 nF. The frequency where the capacitor curve crosses the flat resistor line is the cutoff of the low-pass in the next widget. (2) The inductor rises the other way. (3) Drag the marker to 1 kHz and read Xc and XL.
Design challenge:

At 1 kHz, make the capacitor's reactance match a 1.6 kΩ resistor, and keep the inductor's reactance under 100 Ω so the coil is still a small series term. That crossing is the cutoff of the low-pass in the next widget.

  • Marker at 1 kHz
  • Xc within about 5% of 1.6 kΩ
  • XL under 100 Ω

Produce: R, C, and L. 1.6 kΩ, 100 nF, and 8 mH put Xc at 1.59 kΩ and XL at 50 Ω. Check it in the widget above.

Hint

Xc = 1/(2πfC) and XL = 2πfL. A larger capacitor lowers Xc. Set C so Xc crosses 1.6 kΩ at 1 kHz, which is the same RC product as a 1 kHz low-pass.

Check your design

R = 1.6 kΩ, C = 100 nF, L = 8 mH, marker at 1 kHz. Xc = 1/(2π × 1000 × 100 nF) = 1592 Ω, about 0.5% under 1.6 kΩ. XL = 2π × 1000 × 8 mH = 50.3 Ω, under 100 Ω. The RC cutoff 1/(2π × 1.6 kΩ × 100 nF) is 995 Hz.

Design challenge:

Build a low-pass with cutoff near 1 kHz, using the same 1.6 kΩ and 100 nF that crossed on the reactance plot. At ten times that cutoff the gain should be about a tenth, which is −20 dB.

  • Low-pass, R = 1.6 kΩ, C = 100 nF
  • Cutoff near 1 kHz (995 Hz from 1/(2πRC))
  • At 10× cutoff, |H| near 0.10, about −20 dB

Produce: R, C, and the low-pass choice. Those two parts put fc at 995 Hz, and a decade above it the gain is 0.0995. Check it in the widget above.

Hint

fc = 1/(2πRC). A first-order low-pass falls at 20 dB per decade, so |H| = 1/√(1+(f/fc)²) is about 0.1 when f = 10 fc. Set the marker to 10×.

Check your design

Low-pass, R = 1.6 kΩ, C = 100 nF, marker at 10× cutoff. fc = 1/(2π × 1600 × 100 nF) = 994.7 Hz. At 9947 Hz, |H| = 1/√(1+100) = 0.0995, and 20·log10(0.0995) = −20.0 dB. The widget rounds that magnitude to 0.100 and the decibel readout to −20.0 dB. The −3 dB line stays at fc.
